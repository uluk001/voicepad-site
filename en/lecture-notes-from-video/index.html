Lecture notes and chapters from a recorded lecture or YouTube video

A ninety-minute lecture recorded on a phone, or a whole course sitting on YouTube, and an exam next week. Rewatching everything is not realistic. What helps is a set of notes to study from and a way to jump straight to the part that did not make sense the first time. The Voicepad bot in Telegram turns the recording or the link into a transcript with timestamps and notes with the key points, and for a YouTube link the notes end with chapters and timecodes.

How to get the notes

  1. Open @Voicepad_bot and tap Start.
  2. Send the lecture: an audio or video file, or a YouTube link. VK Video and RuTube links work too. Inside Telegram a file can be up to 20 MB, which is a Telegram limit; a full lecture recording is usually bigger, so upload it on the site. Without a login the site transcribes only the first 3 minutes, so log in with Telegram (one button): files up to 200 MB, and the transcript is saved to your bot history.
  3. Wait for the transcript. Processing time grows with the length; recordings up to 3 hours are accepted.
  4. Tap Summary. You get the key points, and for a YouTube link, a list of chapters with timecodes at the end.

Studying from the transcript

The notes give you the structure; the details live in the full transcript, where every paragraph has a timestamp, so going back to a particular example takes seconds. The Question button answers from the recording: “what did the lecturer say the exam would cover?” or “list the three examples from the second half”. DOCX is handy for a paper, TXT for any notes app, and /history keeps your transcripts for 30 days if you come back to a lecture later.

Recording a lecture yourself

The quality of the notes follows the quality of the audio. A phone on the front desk gives a clean recording of the lecturer and a clean transcript. A phone in the back row, with chatter around it, gives gaps, and some terms will need to be filled in from memory. Anything on the board or on slides is not in the audio, so the notes cover what was said, not what was shown. Recognition runs on ElevenLabs Scribe v2, and the language is detected automatically.

To dictate a summary of your own, use the recorder on the site: it works in the browser, and after a Telegram login a recording can run up to 30 minutes.

Lectures in a study group chat

Add the bot to the group chat. Voice messages and video notes up to three minutes, such as questions before a seminar, are transcribed automatically for everyone. The chat gets 30 free minutes a month, then minutes come from the balance of whoever sent the recording, and an admin can switch automatic transcription off with /mute.

Pricing

15 minutes every month are free. A full lecture does not fit into that, so there are packs: 60 minutes for 199 RUB (one lecture), 300 minutes for 690 RUB (three ninety-minute lectures with room to spare), 1000 minutes for 1990 RUB. Minutes never expire, you pay for the actual length of the recording, and nothing is charged if recognition fails. Pay by card through Tribute with the /buy command in the bot.

FAQ

Are lecture notes free?

15 minutes of recording a month are free, and the Summary button is under every transcript. Enough for a short video or a segment of a lecture; a full one needs a pack.

Will a lecture of an hour and a half work?

Yes, recordings up to 3 hours are accepted, as a file or as a link. A file over 20 MB goes through the site: up to 200 MB after a Telegram login.

Do I get chapters for a phone recording?

Chapters come with YouTube links. A file transcript has timestamps on every paragraph, so you can find the moment in your player by hand.

Can I ask questions about the lecture?

Yes, the Question button in the bot answers from the recording.

Does it work for lectures in Russian?

Yes. The language is detected automatically, Russian and English included.

Do the slides end up in the notes?

No, only speech is transcribed. Keep the slides next to the notes for formulas and diagrams.
