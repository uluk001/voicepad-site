Podcast episode transcript with speakers and timecodes

Show notes need timestamps, the website needs a text version so the episode shows up in search, the newsletter needs two good quotes, and the video version needs captions. Doing all of that by hand for a fifty-minute episode takes an afternoon. The Voicepad bot in Telegram takes the episode file or a link to the published video and returns a transcript where every line has a speaker label and a timecode.

How to get the transcript

  1. Open @Voicepad_bot and tap Start.
  2. Send the episode as a file (MP3, M4A, WAV or the video export), or paste the link if it is already on YouTube and skip the download. Inside Telegram a file can be up to 20 MB, which is a Telegram limit. A full episode is often bigger: upload it on the site. Without a login the site transcribes only the first 3 minutes, so log in with Telegram (one button): files up to 200 MB, and the transcript is saved to your bot history.
  3. Wait. Processing time grows with the length of the episode; recordings up to 3 hours are accepted.
  4. Read the transcript. Host and guest are labelled Speaker 1 and Speaker 2, and every line carries a timecode.

Buttons under the transcript in the bot: Summary (for a YouTube episode it ends with chapters and timecodes), Article for the blog version, Post for the announcement, Question for pulling out one specific moment (“where did the guest mention the book?”), SRT for captions, DOCX and TXT for editing.

Timecodes for show notes

If the episode is on YouTube, send the link and tap Summary: the chapters at the end come as a timecode and a short title, ready to paste into the description. Rename the titles to match your style. If you sent a file, every paragraph of the transcript still has a timecode, so a chapter list is a matter of scrolling through and noting where the topic changes.

Quotes and speaker names

To find a quote, ask the Question button: “what did the guest say about hiring?” returns the answer, and the timecode next to the relevant passage leads to the exact wording in the transcript. Speaker labels are generic, Speaker 1 and Speaker 2, so before publishing replace them with names in one find-and-replace pass in the DOCX. When the host and the guest talk over each other or laugh at the same time, a line boundary can move by a word, which is worth a glance in the liveliest parts of the conversation.

Recognition quality

Podcasts are recorded on decent microphones more often than not, and that is the best case for speech recognition. The bot runs on ElevenLabs Scribe v2, and the language is detected automatically. Still worth checking before publishing: guest names, company and book titles, numbers. A remote guest on a poor connection will produce more errors than a studio recording.

Pricing

15 minutes every month are free. That is enough to test the quality on a segment, not for a full episode. Packs: 60 minutes for 199 RUB, 300 minutes for 690 RUB (about six fifty-minute episodes), 1000 minutes for 1990 RUB. Minutes never expire, you pay for the actual length of the recording, and nothing is charged if recognition fails. Pay by card through Tribute with the /buy command in the bot.

FAQ

Does it tell the host and the guest apart?

Yes, lines are labelled Speaker 1 and Speaker 2 with a timecode on each. Rename the labels in the DOCX before publishing.

Can I get chapters for the show notes?

For an episode on YouTube, tap Summary: chapters with timecodes are at the end. For a file, use the timecodes in the transcript.

Which file formats work?

MP3, M4A, WAV, OGG, MP4, MOV and other audio and video formats. In the bot a file can be up to 20 MB; on the site, up to 200 MB after a Telegram login. Without a login the site takes files up to 50 MB but transcribes only the first 3 minutes.

Is the transcript free?

15 minutes a month are free. A full episode needs a pack, the smallest is 60 minutes for 199 RUB.

Can I get captions for the video version?

Yes, the SRT button under the transcript returns a subtitle file with timestamps.
